The Complete Guide to ATS-Friendly Resume Formatting

Learn how applicant tracking systems parse your resume, and discover the formatting pitfalls that get qualified candidates rejected before a human recruiter even sees them.

What is an Applicant Tracking System (ATS)?

An Applicant Tracking System (ATS) is an automated software application used by over 98% of Fortune 500 corporations and modern tech employers to manage the recruitment and hiring process. When you upload a resume, the ATS extracts the textual content, categorizes the information into structured database fields (Experience, Skills, Education, Contact Details), and scores candidate relevance against the employer's job description.

Major Formatting Traps That Break ATS Parsers

  • Tables and Multi-Column Layouts: Many ATS parsers read resumes from left to right across the full horizontal page width. When multi-column tables are used, text from column one is frequently scrambled together with column two, creating unreadable nonsense in recruiter databases.
  • Text Saved Inside Images or Canvas: Graphic designers often save skill bars, company logos, or even full headers as images. ATS parsers cannot read raster images; to the scanner, those skills simply do not exist.
  • Header & Footer Contact Info: Many legacy document parsers ignore Microsoft Word and PDF headers and footers completely. If your email address and phone number are isolated in the page header, recruiters will receive an empty profile with no way to contact you.
  • Fancy Non-Standard Icons for Bullets: Using stylized graphical glyphs instead of standard circular bullets or dashes can confuse character encoding engines and insert garbage characters into your text.

Recommended Section Headers

Stick to universally recognizable section titles so automated categorizers properly map your achievements:

  • • Technical Skills (Languages, Frameworks, Developer Tools, Databases)
  • • Professional Experience or Software Engineering Experience
  • • Projects (Technical Projects, Open Source Contributions)
  • • Education (Degree, Major, Institution, Graduation Year)
